Life is without inherent meaning. One can ascertain and strangle the concept of existence to pull out something of value and contort that into one's realm of sensation and the best part about that is, so can anyone else. This notion precisely led to the existence of myriad religions, myths, superstitions, and all of them lack a common function; the ability to show evidence-based replicable results.

Fake news is a common problem in current society. We come across dozens of fabricated articles every day that are just designed to create hype and attract an audience. Some of those articles are so poorly written and hence fail to deceive the reader, yet there are several articles out there that are skillfully crafted and captivate the reader into believing the key points. Checking for the veracity of every single source of information is an insurmountable task even with enough computing power and machinery, thus the least we could do to weed out the false information is to read the article with an objective lens and an unbiased mind, lest you are just reading the article to reinforce existing valid or invalid biases. The same critical lens we readily apply to a dubious news headline is the one we rarely turn on our oldest and most deeply held beliefs.

## Growing Up Between Two Worlds

Growing up in a traditional Hindu Brahmin family, I am aware of the bias developed in young children about Brahmin superiority and not to question religious teachings and rituals, since those are the fundamental truths of this universe. I lived several years of my childhood blindly believing in this story since my parents, extended family, family friends cherished these beliefs. The positive reinforcement provided to a child's brain during its formative years is crucial since it defines the world view of the individual.

Brahmins form the apex of the caste system in India and being born a Brahmin was supposed to be a gift of God. This superiority complex monopolized the right of reading holy scriptures and interpreting them to this specific caste of people. The advantage I personally derived from this complex was that my family placed a keen focus on academic merit and rigorous studying. But instead of studying the Vedas and Upanishads, I was admitted to an English-medium school that taught the English alphabet and scientific theories rather than divine Hindu knowledge.

This was acceptable to my family circle since no one questioned sending children to English-medium schools in the early 21st century. This first step towards scientific theory in my life can be attributed to British colonial rule in India, who brought their heavy cultural influences onto the Indian public along with their avarice and machinery.

## The Religious and the Objectivist

Years rolled by slowly and two opposing characteristics grew in me: the religious and the objectivist characteristics. The religious characteristic was reinforced by family and relatives that strengthened my belief that the purpose of life was to work hard, marry well, have a child, support the family and pray for betterment in current life and act accordingly so as to avoid the cycle of births and deaths.

The objectivist characteristic was the one I developed on my own from textbook learnings and online resources. This was a very fragile yet powerful driving force in me. I personally liked this characteristic since it was rapidly evolving with more information and knowledge with a strong scientific basis. Whenever violations from this were observed there was always a compelling rational explanation, whereas whenever violations were observed in religious characteristics, there was a compelling but highly irrational explanation attached.

It was only during high school that I started to notice the cognitive dissonance slowly building up in me. The objectivist characteristic made me question every decision I made, every thought I had, every action I observed and tried to fit into the complex workings of the real world. I was having an existential crisis, and this kept me awake several nights. Sharing my concerns with school friends did not yield much help since most of them were not concerned about the bigger mysteries of life yet. I was left to my own mental faculties to figure this out.

## Investigating the Origin Stories

On deeper exploration into my religion of Hinduism, the internet came up with a variety of results showing grandiose theories of creation of life. None of them seemed to convince me. My next step was to investigate other religions I had heard of from classroom teaching and friends, those were primarily Christianity, Islam, Buddhism and Jainism. The stories of divine creation differed widely between the religions. To the mind of a 12-year-old, some of them were exciting, some scary and some seemed ludicrous. Navigating through these mystical accounts put me under immense pressure since I was unsure which story was true and which was not. Surely not all of these stories could be true. It shocked me when I realized that there were millions of people around the world who believed in their own version of the story. But how could they all be correct? At this point I realized that religious origin stories are fictional narratives that are intended to captivate the people and force them into cooperation. It is easier for everyone to believe a common storyline than for every individual to work out the philosophical roots of our existence. At the same time, the common storyline that unites us should not overpower our rational mind. We should acknowledge that stories bind us together and aid us in working together efficiently, but that story is not the end goal or source of meaning.

## The Void and the Story

It is difficult for the masses to accept this and move forward since the difficult part of this exchange is there is no alternate meaning-instilling story to offer in return. After the First World War ended and Germany was stripped of all its military prowess and lands, they were left to develop for themselves. This heightened the ultranationalist movements and it was the void that brought the Germans together that triggered the Second World War. One could naturally ask, "Why wasn't there a Third World War a few years after Germany and Japan lost the Second World War?". It was because rather than just stripping the country's military power and lands, the Allies established democracies in Germany and Japan and ensured that a sustainable government was in place.

Coming back to the discussion on religion, pragmatists and scientists have nothing to offer in return to religious zealots other than that our existence is nihilistic and meaningless in the context of the entire cosmos. We are not born to achieve and work towards an inherent purpose. Humans try to ascertain meaning to life so that they have a reason to live, since if there were no reason to meaningfully live, what could be wrong with jumping off a cliff?

If life has no meaning for the individual, then the next best thing people cling on to is collectivism. "The primary goal of my life is to help others". If that were true, then what is the reason for the existence of "other" people in the first place? This recursive reasoning does not lead to a viable solution since our altruism only exists in rationality and withers down as soon as we face a life-altering decision. Most of us do not believe in 90% of the gods the world has offered, yet we strongly hold on to a special one or special group of gods. Why?

## The Evolution of Belief

From historical accounts gathered over centuries we notice that different civilizations tried to answer the question of existence in their unique way. Chinese and Indian civilizations considered the animal spirits to be deities. Migration patterns of certain birds, animals would predict the natural cycle of events. With current scientific research, we are aware that changes in the environment cause migration and not the other way around. Yet this would be difficult for primordial humans to observe and they concluded the opposite i.e. the animals possess cosmic powers because they possess the power to change nature.

The belief slowly changed as humans learnt more about animal behavior and biology. As we evolved and learned more about animals, most humans updated their beliefs that animals are not omniscient creatures. Yet there are few people around the world who strongly believe in this and are left behind as humanity progresses. The inference from this is that it is in the best interest of people to experiment with a theory and if it does not fit what we observe and experience universally, it is best to move ahead rather than cling on to outdated beliefs.

After this, people came up with the theory of heaven in the sky and hell beneath the ground. As soon as our knowledge in geography grew and we learnt more about outer space, these theories too slowly started to crumble. The religious denizens then claimed these were just metaphors that existed in a completely different plane of existence. Just a little thinking reveals that this excuse of "plane of existence" could be used for anything.

As soon as I realized that I grew up in an environment that held outdated beliefs, I decided to seek the world on my own and move on rather than cling on. I started to evaluate all the misguided biases, unilateral thinking styles, outrageous moral principles instilled in me. Unfortunately, I could not shake it off all at once. It is not easy for the mind to snap back all the neural connections developed over the years in an instant. The brain is not easily electronically reprogrammable like a computer RAM, at least not yet.

## Exploration vs Exploitation

Humans are gifted with the ability to be exceptionally efficient data processors. We deal with petabytes of visual and auditory data every single day and yet consume much less power compared to a supercomputer. But animals and insects perform this feat too. However, this is not our only superpower. Our power to think long-term and rationally also defines us. Daniel Kahneman, in his book [Thinking Fast and Slow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thinking,_Fast_and_Slow), writes about two thinking styles in our brain: the fast brain and slow brain. The fast brain takes care of activities such as looking at a food item and classifying it as edible or not. Essentially our emotion and gut feeling comes into play here. The slow brain is the sensible and rational brain that follows a step-by-step process rather than jumping around. An example of this is to calculate how to split the bill among friends after a dinner.

These traits help one thrive in this world and hence trusting your sense and rationality is the only certain action one can perform. Our actions are hence causally related to the exposure of our decision-making process. Let us take an example, Sam visits the closest grocery store (store A) to purchase a carton of milk for $10. Charlie purchases milk from store B for $20. If Sam and Charlie were to meet and exchange information about their milk prices, surely Charlie would be willing to explore store A. If store A happens to be just a little further down from store B, surely Charlie would purchase milk from store A, thus exploiting the gained knowledge.

This problem of exploration vs exploitation is a famous research problem in the field of artificial intelligence too. Several parallels can be drawn to real life from this. As children, we are more inclined to explore and absorb new information around us since our knowledge of the world is limited. As we grow, the balance slowly starts shifting towards exploiting what we already know to have a successful life. We try to exploit our knowledge gained in school and college, at our workplace for a living. It is also the same reason why older people are less incentivized to try out new things than younger folks, since the balance slowly shifts towards exploitation as one's knowledge grows. Too much exploration and we lose too much time in life, too much exploitation and society will say we are missing out on things in life. Each one of us has established our unique tradeoff between exploration and exploitation. Companies face similar problems every day where they can invest more into research or invest more into marketing their current products better. You can read in depth about achieving a perfect balance in the book [Loonshots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loonshots) by Safi Bahcall, where the author eloquently uses the physics of phase transitions to achieve the Bush-Vail balance.

## The Rational Imperative

The conclusion from this is that humans have this unique ability to explore and exploit knowledge and derive fascinating conclusions about the underlying science of the world, and hence we should perform that dutifully. Take in valid information, make sure we process the information in the right manner and assimilate the information in our existing knowledge graph. Deviation of a human to add any information into their mind that does not fit into their foundational framework is a disgrace to one's own intellect and undermines our capability to think.

The values and opinions we hold could be incorrect because of lack of information, but once that new information comes to light, we should be amenable to embrace it, if it offers a parsimonious explanation and not outright reject it just because it contradicts existing values. For instance, children believe in Santa Claus, yet as one grows older people realize the folklore and accept that there is no mystical figure that provides gifts to children. Imagine if children grew up and vehemently fought with their parents about the existence of Santa Claus and continued to believe in him, even though the parents came clean that it was them who had the presents ready. Who is more ignorant: the people who accept the one true god without evidence or the people who reject the one true god due to lack of evidence? Bottom line, use the Santa Claus stories as a bootstrapping mechanism for kids and ease them into rational thinking. Stories and myths do have their value, but teach the limit of their values as well, so we are in control of them and not the other way around.

At the time of my writing this essay, the world is facing a crisis due to the outbreak of COVID-19. Xenophobia runs high during this stressful period. People want to assign meaning to this pandemic and reasons such as "cleansing of humans based on their sins" are making its rounds in society. Ignoring the literate population is causing more harm to the situation than illiteracy. It is precisely this immature sense to find meaning and fabricate a storyline that resonates with millions. This ability to connect with the storyline, if not operated wisely, causes horrendous events to occur such as the past wars such as the Crusades, the current wars against ISIS and terrorism and future wars too.

It was maybe Kierkegaard who said, "The most painful state of being is remembering the future, particularly the one you will never have". This thought should resonate with all of us. It should remind us to take appropriate action in the present based on knowledge acquired in the past to not regret missing out on a great future.
